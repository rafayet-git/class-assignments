An elementary matrix is a matrix obtained by a elementary row operation on I. Hence, there are 3 types. As we'll see, multiplying a matrix A on the left by E produces the same row operation on A that was done on I to produce E.

Example:

$$
E = 
\begin{bmatrix}
  0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&1
\end{bmatrix}\hspace{10cm}\\[10px]

\begin{bmatrix}
  0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&1
\end{bmatrix} \underrightarrow{R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2} \begin{bmatrix}
1&0&0\\  
0&1&0\\
0&0&1
\end{bmatrix} \hspace{10cm}
$$

Type 2: Obtained from I by multiplying a row by a non-zero number

Type 3: obtained by adding a multiple of one row of I to another row.

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix}
1&-4&6\\  
3&0&5\\
4&2&1
\end{bmatrix}
B = \begin{bmatrix}
1&-4&6\\  
0&12&-13\\
4&2&1
\end{bmatrix}

$$

Thm = An elementary matrix is nonsingular and its inverse is of the same type.

Proof = Say E swaps Ri + Rj. Then E^-1 = E.

Say E is obtained by multiplying a row by a. then E' multiplies the same row by 1/a.

Say E is obtained by adding n times row i to row j. then E^-1 adds -m times row i to row j.

Definition (Row equivalence). A is row equivalent to B if there exist E1,E2,...EK such that A=EK...E1B

Theorem 

1. If A is row equivalent to B, B is row equivalent to A.

2. If A is row equivalent to B and B is row equivalent to C, then A is row equivalent to C.

The folllowing are equivalent:

1. A is nonsinglular.

2. Ax=o has only the trivial solution o

3. A is row equivalent to I.

Corollay: Ax=b has a unique soln if and only if A is nonsingular.

Consequences:

1. All nonsingular nxn matrices are row equivalent since they are all row equivalent to E.

2. Every nonsingular matrix can be written as a product of elemetary matrices since A is row equivalent to I, A=EK..E1I=EK..E1

The theorem also gives us a procedure to find A^-1. Since I is row equivalent to A, Ek..E1A= I implies Ek..E1I = A^-1

In words, the sequence of elementary row operations that are done on A to produce I can simultaneously be performed on I to produce A^-1. 

In practice, we augment A with I (A|I)+ the row operations that are done of A to produce I are done at the same time to transform I to A^-1 (I|A^-1)
