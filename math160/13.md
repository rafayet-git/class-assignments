## 3.2 subspaces

Some subsets of a vector space are themselves vector spaces under the operations inherited from V. For a subset S of a vector space V to be a vbector space, it must be closed under scalar multiplication and vector addition.

**Definition**: Substace

Let S be a nonempty subset of a vector space V and suppose the following conditions hold.

1. if x in S, ax is in S.

2. if x, y in S, x+y in S.

Then S is a subspace of V.

Axioms 1-8 all hold since they are true for any elements of V.

The zero vector exists since given x in S, 0x = 0. A4 holds since given x in S, (-1)x = -x.

V and {0} are automatically subspaces. Any other subspace is called a proper subspace.

As part of the definition of subspace, we must know that the set S is nonempty. We usually show this by showing 0 is in S. To show we have a subspace:

1. 0 is in S

2. If x in S, ax is in S.

3. if x, y in S, x+y in S.

Example: Lines through the origin in R2 form subspaces, such as y = 2x: [x 2x]

$$
S = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix}
x\\
2x+1
\end{bmatrix} \mid x \text{ is a real number} \right\}
$$

Null space of a matrix

Let A be a mxn m0atrix and N(A) The solutions to the homogenous system Ax=0. That is, NA = {x in Rn | Ax=0}. Then NA is a subspace of Rn.

1. 0 in NA since A0=0

2. Given X in NA, A(ax) = aA(x) = a0 = 0 

3. Given x,y in NA, A (x+y) = Ax+Ay = 0+0=0

Definition (Linear combination span)

Let V1,V2,...Vn be vectors in a vector space V. A sum, of the form a1V1+a2V2+...+anVn is called a linear combination of the vectors V1,V2,...,Vn.

The set of all linear combinations of V1,V2,Vn is called the span of V1,V2,...,Vn and is written Span(V1,V2,...Vn).

NA = Span([1,-2,1,0]T , [-1,1,0,1]T)
