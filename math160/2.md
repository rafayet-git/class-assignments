The 3 operations that produce equivalent linear systems can be translated to operations on an augmented matrix.

### Elementary row operations

1. interchange row i, j $(R_i \leftrightarrow R_j)$

2. Multiply row i by a nonzero $\alpha$ $(R_i \rightarrow \alpha R_i)$

3. Replace row i by the sum of itself and $\alpha$ times row j $(R_i \rightarrow R_i+\alpha R_j)$

Ex: Solve the system

$$
\begin{cases}
x_1+2x_2-3x_3 = -4 \\
x_1+4x_2-2x_3 = 6 \\
3x_1 -2x_2+2x_3=8
\end{cases}
$$

Solution:

$$
\left[
\begin{array}{ccc|c}
  1&2&-3&-4\\
  1&4&-2&6\\
   3&-2&2&8
\end{array}
\right]
R_2 - R_1, R_3-3R_1
\\
\left[
\begin{array}{ccc|c}
  1&2&-3&-4\\
  0&2&1&10\\
   0&-8&11&20
\end{array}
\right]
R_3+4R_2
\\
\left[
\begin{array}{ccc|c}
  1&2&-3&-4\\
  0&2&1&10\\
   0&0&15&60
\end{array}
\right]
\\
15x_3 = 60 \rightarrow x_3 = 4
\\2x_2 + 4 = 10 \rightarrow x_2 =3
\\x_1 + 6 - 9 = -4 \rightarrow x_1 = -1
$$

The coefficient matrix is said to be in strict upper trianglular form: The diagonals are nonzero, and 0's appear below the diagonal.

<u>Application</u>: Linear programming in the branch of mathematics that asks you to maximize or minimize a linear quanitity subject to linear conditions

Example: A manufacturer produces two items, x and y. He makes \$2 on each x and \$1 for y. How much of each must be produced to earn \$16, if 12 items are produced?

$$
x+y=12
\\2x+y = 16
\\x = 4, y = 8
$$

Costs \$0.2 and 12 minutes to produce x and \$0.4 and 3 minutes to produce y. \$4  and 120 minutes is available to produce both.

$$

\text{Profit} & 2 & 1 &

$$

### Row echelon form

Not all nxn coefficent matrices can be put into strict upper triangular form.

$$
\left[
\begin{array}{cccc|c}
  1&1&3&2&4\\
  0&0&-6&-1&-7\\
  0&0&7&4&12\\
  0&0&-1&-2&1 
\end{array}
\right]
$$

However we can put this coeffient matrix + the coefficient matrix of mxn linear systems (even when m != n) so that the soln set is easily determined

<u>Row echelon form </u>

A matrix is in row echelon form if:

1. The first nonzero entry in any nonzero row is 1, called a leading 1.

2. The leading 1 on any row lies to the right of the leading one on the preceding row

3. Any row consisting of all 0's lie below the non zero rows
