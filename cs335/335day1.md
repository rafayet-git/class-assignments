### Speed of algorithim vs growth rate

    - Faster asymptotic growth rate = higher runtime - slower algorithim
    - Slower asymptotic growth rate = lower runtime = faster/ more efficient algorithm
    - So O(n) can be described as faster than o(n^2) but slower-growing than it.

- o is slower growing, $\omega$ is faster growing, $\theta$ is same asymptotic growth rate.
- O is an upper bound "no slower than", $\Omega$ is a lower bound "can't do better than

- Continuous random variable
- Ex: Suppose individuals with a certain gene have a 0.6 probability of eventually contracting a certain disease. 12 individuals with this gene participate in a life time study. What is the probability that 8 contract the disease?
  - x = Number of people out of 12, n = 12, p=0.6.
  - P(X=8) = $\binom{12}{8} 0.6^8 * 0.4^4$
  - P(X=0) = $\binom{12}{0} 0.6^0 * 0.4^12 = 0.00001677$
- Mean/expected value, variance, and SD of binomial RV
  - E(X) = $\mu_x = np$
  
  - Var(x) = $\sigma^2_x = np(1-p)$
